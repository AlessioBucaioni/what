# Interview guide

These questions are shortened as presented in the manuscript. The interviews used the guide flexibly; the table does not imply that every question was asked verbatim in every session.

## Initial questions

Q1. How would you describe your current role as a software architect?

Q2. When did GenAI first affect your work, directly or through surrounding engineering work? What changed first?

Q3. Describe a recent example of GenAI affecting architecture or related engineering work. What changed for you?

Q4. What has changed most in your role since GenAI adoption, and what has remained unchanged?

Q5. Has GenAI changed how you interact with other roles and stakeholders?

Q6. Has GenAI changed the boundary between architects' work and that of other roles?

Q7. Has GenAI changed your responsibilities, decision authority, or accountability?

Q8. Has GenAI changed what architecture artifacts or knowledge mean for your role?

Q9. What makes architects valuable now compared with before GenAI adoption?

Q10. What responsibilities or expectations are emerging for architects, and which are becoming less central?

Q11. What will architects do in three to five years, and what will receive more or less attention?

Q12. Beyond your predictions, what should the architect role become in GenAI-intensive development?

Q13. Under what conditions would the role evolve differently?

Q14. What important change or continuity in the architect role have we missed?

## Refinement after I6

Following the first preliminary assessment after I6, we retained Q1 for background, prioritized Q6–Q10 and added Q15 on developing architectural judgment.

Q15. How can architects develop judgment if junior engineers use GenAI for work they previously learned by doing?

## Refinement after I9

Following the second assessment after I9, Q16–Q21 became the focus of later interviews, alongside Q1.

Q16. Has GenAI shifted work from architects to other roles?

Q17. Has GenAI changed your responsibilities, authority, or accountability?

Q18. Describe a case where GenAI reduced or bypassed the architect's role, or enabled others to make architectural decisions.

Q19. Some participants see faster, higher-volume GenAI output increasing architects' importance. When might the opposite occur, and why?

Q20. Could architecture remain important while the architect role fades?

Q21. What conditions could change this evolution?

Q19 was asked only after participants had expressed their spontaneous views.
